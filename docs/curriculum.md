# A1 Tagalog Curriculum

This is the authoring contract for **My Tagalog Workbook**. It defines what A1 means here, the 36-lesson path, the lesson template, the assessment plan and the grammar scope. Content that does not fit this document should be treated as A2 or later.

**Audience:** one learner, Spanish L1, English L2, beginner in Tagalog. English is the main explanation language; Spanish is used for contrasts, cognates and false-friend warnings.

**Pronunciation style rule:** anchor pronunciation explanations in Spanish first — the five vowels, the `r` flap, `ñ`, and the `h`/`j` contrast. English comparisons are secondary and should appear only when they add something the Spanish speaker cannot already infer. Do not write objectives such as “pronounce without English long-vowel glides” as the primary frame; say “use the Spanish vowel values as the base” and mention English as a secondary caution.

**Goal:** a functional A1 exit profile, not an official CEFR certificate.

---

## 1. A1 exit profile

By the end of the workbook, the learner can:

1. Read the Filipino alphabet and pronounce familiar Tagalog words with the correct vowels, `ng`, stress and glottal stop.
2. Greet, take leave and use `po` / `opo` appropriately with strangers, elders and people in authority.
3. Introduce themselves and ask or answer name, origin, residence, age and nationality.
4. Use `ang` / `si` / `sina`, `ng` / `ni` / `nina` and `sa` / `kay` / `kina` to mark the topic, possessor/actor and location/recipient.
5. Use nominative, ng-form and sa-form pronouns, including `kami` vs `tayo` and `ikaw` vs `ka`.
6. Count, use numbers for prices, time and age, and ask `Magkano?`
7. Ask and answer simple questions with `ano`, `sino`, `saan`, `kailan`, `bakit`, `paano`, `ilan` and `alin`.
8. Use `may` / `wala` / `mayroon`, `ito` / `iyan` / `iyon`, the `na` / `-ng` linker and common `ma-` adjectives.
9. Use common actor-focus verbs in completed, incomplete and contemplated aspects, and recognize a small set of object-focus phrases.
10. Use `gusto`, `ayaw`, `kailangan` and `puwede` with `ng`, a linker or a verb; make polite requests.
11. Tell the time, days and dates; describe a daily routine and hobbies; talk about the weather.
12. Order food, shop, ask prices and handle simple quantities.
13. Ask for directions, use transport and ask for help in an emergency.
14. Invite someone, accept or decline, and make simple plans.
15. Understand short, slow dialogues and simple texts; write a 5–8 sentence message about a familiar topic.
16. Recognize Spanish loanwords and cognates, and avoid the most common false friends.

## 2. Unit and lesson map

Six units, six lessons each, 36 lessons. Each lesson is designed for 20–30 minutes of core study plus 10 minutes of review.

### Unit 1 — Sounds & Survival

| # | Lesson | Objectives | Core language |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sounds and spelling | Read the alphabet; pronounce the five vowels; recognize `ng`; hear stress and glottal stop | Vowels, consonants, `ng`, abakada vs modern alphabet, stress, glottal stop |
| 2 | Greetings and politeness | Greet by time; ask how someone is; use `po`/`opo`; thank and say goodbye | `Magandang umaga/hapon/gabi`, `Kumusta ka?`, `Mabuti naman`, `Salamat`, `Walang anuman`, `Paalam`, `po`/`opo` |
| 3 | Names and introductions | Introduce yourself; ask name, origin, residence, age | `Ako si…`, `Ang pangalan ko ay…`, `Taga-saan ka?`, `Taga-Espanya ako`, `Nakatira ako sa…`, `Ilang taon ka na?` |
| 4 | People and pronouns | Use ang pronouns; distinguish `ikaw`/`ka` and `kami`/`tayo`; use `si`/`sina` and `mga` | `ako`, `ikaw`/`ka`, `siya`, `kami`, `tayo`, `kayo`, `sila`; `si`/`sina`; `ang mga` |
| 5 | Numbers and money | Count 0–100; use native and Spanish-derived numbers; ask prices and age | `isa`–`sampu`, `labing-isa`, `dalawampu`, `isandaan`; `uno`–`diyes`; `Magkano?`, `piso`, `sentimo` |
| 6 | Survival phrases and review | Ask for repetition and clarification; say you do not understand | `Hindi ko maintindihan`, `Pakiulit`, `Paano sabihin ang…?`, `Dahan-dahan lang`, `Puwede ba…?` |

**Unit 1 can-do:** introduce myself, greet politely, count and survive a slow conversation.

### Unit 2 — People & Things

| # | Lesson | Objectives | Core language |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ang`, `ng`, `sa` | Identify the topic; use `ang`/`ang mga`, `ng` and `sa`; avoid “ang = the” | `Kumain ang lalaki ng tinapay.` `Pumunta ako sa palengke.` `Nagbigay ako ng libro sa bata.` |
| 2 | `si`, `ni`, `kay` | Use personal markers and their plural forms | `Si Ana ang guro.` `Binili ni Ana ang libro.` `Pumunta kami kay Lola.` `Kumain sina Ana at Ben.` |
| 3 | `ito`, `iyan`, `iyon`; `may`, `wala`, `mayroon` | Point to things; say something exists or does not exist; ask `May… ba?` | `Ito ang libro.` `May kape ba?` `Wala akong pera.` `Mayroon ba kayong tubig?` |
| 4 | Adjectives and the linker | Use common `ma-` adjectives; link with `na`/`-ng`; intensify with `napaka-` | `magandang bahay`, `mabait na bata`, `malaking aso`, `Napakaganda!` |
| 5 | Possession | Use `ko`/`mo`/`niya` and the `sa`-form possessives; talk about family | `Ang bahay ko`, `Ang aking pamilya`, `Ito ang libro niya`; family vocabulary |
| 6 | Descriptions and family | Describe age, appearance and personality; talk about family | `Siya ay dalawampu’t limang taon.` `Mabait ang nanay ko.` `May dalawa akong kapatid.` |

**Unit 2 can-do:** describe people, family and belongings.

### Unit 3 — Food & Market

| # | Lesson | Objectives | Core language |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Food and meals | Name common food and meals; use table phrases | `kanin`, `ulam`, `tubig`, `kape`, `tsaa`, `manok`, `baboy`, `isda`, `gulay`, `prutas`; `Kain na!`, `Kumain ka na ba?` |
| 2 | Actor-focus verbs and aspects | Use `-um-`, `mag-` and common `ma-` verbs in three aspects | `Kumain ako ng tinapay.` `Kumakain ako ng tinapay.` `Kakain ako ng tinapay.` |
| 3 | Object focus intro | Recognize object focus; understand the topic shift; use fixed phrases | `Kinain ko ang tinapay.` `Binili ko ang libro.` `Ginawa ko ang proyekto.` |
| 4 | `gusto`, `ayaw`, `kailangan`, `puwede` | Express wants, dislikes, needs, ability and permission | `Gusto ko ng kape.` `Gusto kong kumain.` `Ayaw ko ng asukal.` `Kailangan ko ng tulong.` `Puwede ba akong umupo?` |
| 5 | Ordering and shopping | Order food; ask prices; buy quantities; bargain politely | `Pahingi ng…`, `Isa pong…`, `Magkano po ito?`, `Tawad naman po.`, `Isang kilo ng…` |
| 6 | Food culture and review | Understand meals, merienda, baon and fiesta; review the unit | Culture reading and unit quiz |

**Unit 3 can-do:** order a meal, shop and ask prices.

### Unit 4 — Daily Life & Time

| # | Lesson | Objectives | Core language |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Time | Ask and tell the time; use morning, afternoon and evening | `Anong oras na?` `Alas-dos na.` `Alas-dos y medya.` `ng umaga/hapon/gabi`, `tanghali`, `hatinggabi` |
| 2 | Days, months and dates | Name days and months; ask and give dates | `Lunes`–`Linggo`, `Enero`–`Disyembre`, `Anong araw ngayon?`, `Anong petsa ngayon?`, `Ika-… ng …` |

| 5 | Hobbies and abilities | Talk about hobbies and what you can do | `Mahilig akong…`, `Marunong akong…`, `Gusto kong…`, `Hindi ako marunong…` |
| 6 | Weather and review | Describe the weather; make small talk; review the unit | `Mainit`, `Malamig`, `Maaraw`, `Mahangin`, `Maulap`, `Umuulan`, `Anong panahon ngayon?` |

**Unit 4 can-do:** describe a day, hobbies and the weather.

### Unit 5 — Getting Around

| # | Lesson | Objectives | Core language |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Places and location | Name places; say where something is; ask `Saan ang…?` | `nasa`, `sa`, `may`; `malapit`, `malayo`; `bahay`, `paaralan`, `trabaho`, `simbahan`, `palengke`, `ospital` |
| 2 | Directions and imperatives | Give and follow simple directions | `kaliwa`, `kanan`, `deretso`, `liko`, `kanto`, `tawid`; `Kumanan ka.` `Lumiko ka sa kanan.` |

## 3. Lesson template

Every lesson uses the same sections. The content file contains data; the renderer owns layout.

1. **Header**
   - Unit, lesson number, title.
   - Estimated time (20–30 minutes).
   - 1–2 measurable objectives.
   - A can-do statement in learner language.

2. **Warm-up** (2–3 minutes)
   - 2–3 review questions from earlier lessons.
   - One question that activates prior knowledge.

3. **Input**
   - A short explanation (150–300 words) with positive, negative and question examples.
   - An English explanation; Spanish contrast only where it adds value.
   - One “why this matters” sentence.

4. **Vocabulary** (12–20 items)
   - Tagalog, English, part of speech.
   - Example sentence and English translation.
   - Audio key.
   - Optional Spanish cognate or false-friend note.
   - No more than 20 items; split the lesson if the list grows.

5. **Pattern**
   - One grammar point or sentence pattern.
   - Positive, negative and question forms.
   - 3–5 examples with translations.
   - One common mistake and its correction.

6. **Dialogue** (6–10 lines)
   - Two or three speakers.
   - Natural, level-appropriate Tagalog.
   - Audio, replay and slow speed.
   - Translation toggle.
   - One comprehension question.

7. **Guided practice** (8–12 exercises)
   - At least five exercise types.
   - Sequence: recognition → controlled production → free production.
   - At least one listening item and one production item.
   - Every item has an answer key, accepted alternatives and an explanation.

8. **Culture or Spanish note**
   - One short, specific note.
   - No stereotypes, no “exotic” framing.
   - Spanish notes focus on cognates, sound contrasts, false friends or grammar contrasts.

9. **Can-do check** (5–8 questions)
   - 80% to complete.
   - Unlimited retries.
   - Wrong answers link to the section where the point was taught.

10. **Review**
    - The lesson’s vocabulary and patterns enter the SRS queue.
    - The next lesson is one click away.
    - The lesson appears as complete in progress.

### Authoring checklist

- [ ] Objectives are measurable.
- [ ] Can-do statement is in learner language.
- [ ] Vocabulary count is 12–20.
- [ ] Every example is verified.
- [ ] Audio key exists for every word and dialogue line.
- [ ] At least five exercise types are present.
- [ ] Every exercise has an answer key and explanation.
- [ ] One common mistake is addressed.
- [ ] One Spanish note or culture note is included.
- [ ] Lesson check has 5–8 questions.
- [ ] Flashcards are generated or referenced.
- [ ] No A2 content leaked in.
- [ ] `npm run content:check` passes.

## 4. Grammar scope

### Taught at A1

- Predicate-first sentences and basic `ay` inversion.
- `ang` / `ang mga`, `si` / `sina`, `ng` / `ng mga`, `ni` / `nina`, `sa` / `sa mga`, `kay` / `kina`.
- Nominative, ng-form and sa-form pronouns; `ikaw` vs `ka`; `kami` vs `tayo`.
- `may`, `wala`, `mayroon`; `ito`, `iyan`, `iyon`.
- `na` / `-ng` linker; common `ma-` adjectives; `napaka-`.
- Actor-focus verbs with `-um-`, `mag-` and common `ma-` verbs; completed, incomplete and contemplated aspects.
- A small set of object-focus forms as fixed phrases: `kinain ko`, `binili ko`, `ginawa ko`.
- `gusto`, `ayaw`, `kailangan`, `puwede` + `ng`, linker or verb.
- Question words and the particle `ba`.
- Common enclitics: `na`, `pa`, `lang`, `po`, `din`/`rin`, `daw`/`raw`.
- Simple imperatives and polite requests with `paki-`.
- Numbers, time, dates, prices and quantities.
- Connectors: `at`, `o`, `pero`, `kasi`, `dahil`.

### Deferred to A2 or later

- Full four-focus production (object, location, beneficiary) as a system.
- Complete affix inventory and verb classes.
- Complex aspect and mood distinctions; `ipag-`, `ipa-`, causative verbs.
- Advanced enclitic ordering.
- Formal/literary Tagalog and deep register variation.
- Taglish code-switching as a productive skill.

### The focus system at A1

Teach it as **“which noun is the topic?”**, not as four conjugation tables:

- `Kumain ako ng tinapay.` — I ate bread. (the actor is the topic)
- `Kinain ko ang tinapay.` — I ate the bread. (the bread is the topic)

Full four-focus tables are an A2 tool. They must not appear in A1 lessons or in the A1 reference.

### Terms to use consistently

| Term | Meaning in this workbook |
|---|---|
| Topic | The noun marked by `ang` / `si` / `sina`; the thing the sentence is about |
| Actor | The person doing the action |
| Object | The thing affected by the action |
| Ng-form pronoun | `ko`, `mo`, `niya`, `namin`, `natin`, `ninyo`, `nila`; used for possessors and non-topic actors |
| Sa-form pronoun | `akin`, `iyo`, `kanya`, `amin`, `atin`, `inyo`, `kanila`; used after `sa`, `para sa`, etc. |
| Aspect | Completed, incomplete or contemplated; not the same as English tense |
| Focus | Which noun is the topic; introduced gradually |

Do **not** call `ko`, `mo`, `niya` “oblique” pronouns. They are ng-form pronouns. The sa-form pronouns are the oblique forms.

## 5. Assessment blueprint

| Assessment | Length | Pass | Retakes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson check | 5–8 questions | 80% | Unlimited | Gates lesson completion |
| Unit quiz | 15–20 questions | 80% | Unlimited | Unlocks after all six lessons; per-tag breakdown |
| Final exam | 50–60 questions | 80% | Unlimited | Listening, reading, grammar/vocabulary, guided production; certificate |
| SRS review | 10 new cards/day, 50 reviews/day | — | — | Four-grade scale mapped to SM-2 |

### Mastery

- A vocabulary card is “known” after three successful reviews with an interval of at least seven days.
- Grammar mastery is estimated from quiz items tagged with that grammar point.
- Mistakes feed a mixed review queue.
- The learner can always see why an item is being reviewed.

### Question design

- One clear correct answer per item, unless the item is explicitly open-ended.
- Distractors should be plausible but unambiguously wrong.
- Avoid trick questions and double negatives.
- Listening items use slow, clear speech and replay.
- Translation items accept legitimate variants; show a model answer and allow self-assessment when needed.
- Every item has an explanation linked to the lesson section.

## 6. Spanish-speaker notes

Every unit includes at least one or two “Desde el español” notes.

### Advantages to use

- Five vowels with stable values, close to Spanish.
- `ñ` already exists in the learner’s alphabet.
- Thousands of Spanish loanwords: `kumusta`, `pero`, `puwede`, `gusto`, `oras`, `numero`, `mesa`, `silya`, `bintana`, `kuwarto`, `kutsara`, `tinidor`, `baso`, `plato`, `karne`, `ensalada`, `asukal`, `kape`, `tsokolate`.
- Spanish-derived numbers are common for time, prices and dates: `alas-dos`, `singkuwenta`, `uno, dos, tres…`.
- The Tagalog `r` is a flap, similar to Spanish single `r`.

### Interference to teach explicitly

- False friends: `siyempre` (of course, not “always”), `siguro` (maybe, not “sure”), `basta` (as long as/just, not “enough”), `gusto` (want/like, not “taste”).
- `puwede` (from Spanish `puede`) is a modal, not a conjugated form of “he/she can”.
- Tagalog has no grammatical gender and no adjective agreement.
- There is no copula in many predicate sentences: `Maganda ang bahay` = “The house is beautiful.”
- `ang` is a topic marker, not the definite article “the”; `ng` is not “of”; `sa` is not a one-to-one “in/at/to”.
- `po` / `opo` and `kayo` for elders are part of the grammar of respect, not optional decorations.
- Spanish `j` is not Tagalog `h`; many Spanish loans were nativized (`Jueves` → `Huwebes`, `vapor` → `bapor`).

Notes are additive. The main explanation stays in English so the workbook also works for an English-speaking learner.

## 7. Time and pacing

| Activity | Time |
|---|---|
| Lesson input, vocabulary and pattern | 10–15 minutes |
| Dialogue and listening | 5 minutes |
| Guided practice | 10 minutes |
| Lesson check | 3–5 minutes |
| SRS review | 10 minutes/day |
| Unit quiz | 20–30 minutes |
| Final exam | 45–60 minutes |

A sustainable plan is one lesson every two or three days, with daily review. The dashboard should recommend the next action, not a rigid calendar.
